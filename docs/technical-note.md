# EU AI Act ontology: proof-of-concept technical note

**Source and scope.** This prototype maps the English consolidated text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 dated 27 July 2026 (CELEX `02024R1689-20260727`). It covers all ten Article 5(1) practice headings (including the 2026 points (ba) and (bb)), all eight Annex III areas, the Article 6 high-risk classification routes, provider duties in Article 16, selected deployer duties in Article 26, selected transparency duties in Article 50, Article 53(1) general-purpose model duties, and one governance provision in Article 70. It is a navigational model, not a legal applicability engine. The original 2024 Official Journal text is retained locally for comparison; the dated consolidated version grounds the records.

**Representation.** `AISystem`, `AIPractice`, `ActorRole`, and their categories are OWL classes. Article 5 headings are subclasses of `Article5ListedPractice`; their prohibition conditions are represented by cited statement individuals, not unconditional subclass axioms. `LegalStatement` has subclasses `Obligation`, `Prohibition`, and `ClassificationRule`; each statement is an OWL individual. Its actor, system and practice targets are annotation properties pointing to class IRIs, while `prov:wasDerivedFrom` connects it to a `LegalProvision` individual and `inActVersion` connects the provision to the dated Act individual. Labels, source references, evidence excerpts, conditions and exceptions are queryable annotations. This hybrid preserves a lightweight OWL 2 EL class hierarchy while avoiding the false inference that every Annex III-listed system is high-risk: Article 6(3) provides a derogation. It also means annotation links are retrieved by SPARQL, not inferred by ELK. `GeneralPurposeAIModel` is separate from `AISystem`.

**Competency questions and executable queries.** The four saved SPARQL files in `queries/` answer:

1. `cq1.rq`: Which Article 5 practices have prohibition statements, with what conditions, exceptions and source points?
2. `cq2.rq`: Which Annex III areas are listed in the high-risk route, where are they listed, and what qualification is recorded? The query reports listed categories, not a decision that a particular system is high-risk.
3. `cq3.rq`: Which cited high-risk duties concern providers and deployers? This prototype covers Article 16 and selected Article 26 paragraphs.
4. `cq4.rq`: Which recorded statements have conditions or exceptions, with source references?

Run `python scripts/query.py cq1` (or `cq2`–`cq4`). `python scripts/ontology.py validate` runs all four against expected minimum rows and references in `queries/expected.json`.

**AI-assisted workflow and review.** The local `pi` client called `borg/qwen3.8-27b` with offline mode to propose structured candidates from bounded Article/Annex text. Prompts and raw responses are saved separately; `ontology/reviewed-records.json` is the curated input to the deterministic mOWL/OWLAPI builder. Review corrected source wording, attached important Article 5(1a) and Article 6(3) qualifications, and rejected automatic promotion of Annex III categories to `HighRiskAISystem` subclasses. Raw model output is not consumed by the builder. Recreate a candidate with, for example, `pi --provider borg --model qwen3.8-27b --offline --no-extensions --no-skills --no-prompt-templates --no-themes --no-session --no-tools -p "$(cat prompts/pi-qwen/art6-prompt.txt)"`; local model availability is required only for rerunning extraction, not for building or testing the curated ontology.

**Validation and metrics.** `scripts/ontology.py all` builds RDF/XML through the mOWL OWLAPI wrapper, reloads the serialized file, checks the OWL 2 EL profile, asks ELK for consistency and unsatisfiable named classes, runs all four SPARQL competency queries using RDFLib, checks the consolidated PDF SHA-256, and matches every evidence excerpt within its cited Article in the extracted PDF text. The resulting `reports/validation.json` reports class/statement/provision counts, query rows, evidence-match rate and any failures. These are structural and traceability checks; they do not establish completeness or legal correctness. The current prototype has 41 classes and 42 statements and passes these automated checks. Four [source-to-query traces](trace-examples.md) show the main reviewed examples. Protégé can open the generated `.owl` file for a visual demonstration of the class hierarchy, individuals, annotations and ELK reasoning; it is not required to reproduce any validation result.

**Limits and next work.** The Article 26 and 50 selections are partial. Conditions and exceptions are human-readable strings rather than formal rules; no system instance receives a legal risk verdict. Article 5 points (ba) and (bb) in the dated consolidation require further specialist legal review before authoritative use. A future branch could model conditions with explicit rule objects, compare later consolidations, broaden actor and duty coverage, and add expert-reviewed gold questions. The Git branch isolates this record-and-query approach for that comparison.
